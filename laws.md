# MIBO Pilot Laws and Claim Registry

**Repository:** `mibo-science/core`  
**Scope:** Exploratory longitudinal claims from the MIBO Pilot  
**Evidence cutoff:** Day 12 — 2026-07-21  
**Verified observations:** 224  
**Last updated:** 2026-08-03

MIBO Pilot laws are named, provisional findings about observable behavior in deployed generative-AI services.

They are not universal laws of nature. Pilot findings are not automatically confirmatory MIBO Core Observatory v1.0 findings.

---

## Status categories

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Candidate | A repeated or theoretically important pattern requiring further observation |
| Confirmed | Supported across repeated observations under the Pilot design |
| Revised | Later evidence requires a more precise formulation |
| Strengthened | Later evidence increases support without changing the core formulation |
| Weakened | The pattern remains possible but is less general or less stable |
| Withdrawn | Later evidence contradicts the proposed law |
| Refuted hypothesis | A tested hypothesis failed |
| Provenance-sensitive anomaly | The supplied record may reflect collection or transcription issues rather than independent behavior |
| Superseded | A later candidate provides a better formulation |

# Established laws and major findings

## Law I — Durable Canonical Cores

**Status:** Confirmed with refinements.

Some queries produce durable cores of products or persons across services and sessions, while peripheral selections and rankings fluctuate.

Examples through Day 12 include:

- q001: Asana, Trello, Notion, and Jira as a durable core; Backlog alternates between universal and non-universal status;
- q002: HubSpot CRM, Zoho CRM, and Pipedrive reached exact-product 4/4 convergence on Days 11–12;
- q005: 松尾豊 remained the only universal cross-system person anchor on Day 12.

**Current formulation:**

> Canonicality is best treated as a layered distribution of durable anchors and variable peripheral slots, not as literal unbroken inclusion of every item.

---

## Law II — Perplexity Multi-Lag URL Stability and Source-Regime Persistence

**Status:** Revised and strengthened after Day 12.

Perplexity source continuity depends on the comparison lag and the source-list regime.

Key evidence:

- Day 10 restored all 45 Day 8 URLs despite only 18/45 immediate retention from Day 9;
- Day 11 shifted every query from 9 terminal sources to 20;
- Day 12 retained 93/100 normalized Day 11 URLs;
- citation bracket numbers changed despite high URL persistence.

**Current formulation:**

> Retrieval-mediated source behavior can appear unstable at one lag, recurrent at another, and highly persistent after a source-list regime transition. Normalized URLs—not citation indices—are the appropriate longitudinal identifiers.

---

## Law III — Day 2 Anomaly

**Status:** Confirmed as a localized anomaly.

The coordinated Day 2 deviations reverted and did not form a stable trend or cycle.

**Methodological lesson:**

> Two points cannot distinguish a trend from a localized common anomaly.

## Law VI — Per-Model Response Signatures

**Status:** Confirmed.

Systems display recurring structural and stylistic signatures, including:

- characteristic caveats and follow-up questions;
- ranked versus use-case organization;
- recurring implementation stacks;
- stable semantic product roles;
- different caution and attribution practices.

Content convergence does not erase model-specific response organization.

## Law VIII — Absence of Direct Academic Citation

**Status:** Confirmed with nuance through Day 12.

No direct peer-reviewed paper or arXiv citation was observed in the verified Pilot responses through Day 12.

Nuance:

- Perplexity sometimes used public-health, government, institutional, or official-company sources;
- q005 researcher selection remained primarily grounded in secondary or tertiary web sources;
- a model may mention research or technical terms without providing a direct scholarly source.

**Current formulation:**

> Expert-like, health, technical, and person-selection outputs can remain ungrounded in direct academic literature even when a search-mediated interface provides many citations.

# Provenance limitations after Day 12

The Pilot claim registry must distinguish behavioral evidence from record-integrity issues.

Known examples:

- Day 11 and Day 12 GPT q004 responses contain `2026-08-03`, postdating their declared observation dates;
- the supplied Day 12 GPT and Claude q003 responses are identical;
- Day 13 currently exists only as a placeholder.

These records remain visible, but date-sensitive or independence-sensitive claims require qualification.

# Promotion rule

A candidate should not become a numbered law solely because it appears twice.

Promotion requires:

1. repeated longitudinal support;
2. prospective survivability where prediction is relevant;
3. stable operational definition;
4. provenance-sufficient observations;
5. explicit consideration of counterexamples;
6. a formulation narrower than the evidence requires, not broader.

# Pilot boundary

Observations before 1 September 2026 belong to the developmental MIBO Pilot.

Pilot laws support protocol development and hypothesis generation. They do not automatically enter the confirmatory MIBO Core Observatory v1.0 analysis.
